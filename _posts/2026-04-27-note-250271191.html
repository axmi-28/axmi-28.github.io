But then, there’s the broader technological question of where AI is leading all of us! What if AI, within a year or two, can produce entire essays, entire speeches, entire presentations, that sounds just as human? Well, then the ranting I’ve done on AI writing slop is almost invalid, a minuscule concern relative to the bigger issues that begin to arise.

And this brings me to an even more personal curiosity for me. I’ve always been fairly interested in computer science, although above all else, I have thought of myself as a reader. I read everything. And recently, after barely peeking in to the the “AI-pilled”, I’ve been jolted enough to want to understand AI, in order to understand the changes I’m seeing. In this journal, at least, it’s something I hope to explore more and more at least. Recently, I’ve been lead to the field of “mechanistic interpretability.” To summarize, it is essentially the very empirical attempt to translate neural networks into something that is human interpretable. For now, it’s not clear to me that progress in these labs (Anthropic leads the field) can keep up in any meaningful way with the pace and resources that creating frontier models are devouring. Although this is not to say that work done by the Anthropic team and others in mechanistic interpretability have not been incredibly interesting and meaningful so far — as much as I might say that if Anthropic was the only corporation developing frontier models, the story would be entirely different for the better. But the reality is that there’s OpenAI, and even worse, there’s Deepseek, Qwen, Kimi. But that’s for another note.

For now, I’ll end on a final observation/curiosity I’ve found — perhaps it is a trick of my relatively shallow knowledge, but I can almost find an unnerving correlation of AI mathematical computations and number planes to human brains. It seems almost plausible, almost reasonable, that human minds have evolved to the level of complexity (and consciousness) to today from much the same principles of which artificial intelligence is trained (albeit with much more simplified incentives). Much like AIs can adopt personas, we can adopt personas. I am finding this hard to articulate now.
