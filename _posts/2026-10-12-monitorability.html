I’ve recently been paying increasing attention to the notion of “AI” in the world around me being profoundly miscalibrated from any sensible notion of “AI-pilledness.” Partly inspired by this recent post, and this tweet.

It is one thing to occasionally scroll past obvious AI-written content on twitter or substack, but it is entirely another thing to be in high school currently. Like, you wouldn’t believe some of the things I’ve seen. I mean teachers, I presume unintentionally, handing out analytical writing assignments on entirely hallucinated text, or most students in class who literally hand any homework they have to an LLM, or class presentations full with AI slides, scripts, and the occasional AI video. Part of this confused state in education, as I perceive it, is the truly unorganized and unprepared administrators involved. The general policy might be that using “generative AI” is “plagiarism” (because “ChatGPT is trained on internet text after all!”), but in practice this works out pretty stupidly. Some teachers, forward thinking enough, correctly work out that “AI is here to stay” so we might as well “learn how to use it productively to enhance our own competence!” I’m using a lot of quotes, because these are tropes I’ve heard a nauseating number of times.

Generally, I still somehow know below a dozen students who would really care about the Astra release, or know what Fable is. And for those that do know or care (mainly programming nerds, see), they would hardly pay attention to “a significant decrease in monitorability.”

And I think this is starting to be concerning, maybe even dangerous - particularly in short-timeline worlds. Currently, the majority of the public, the youth in particular, see AI as this “derpy chatbot” for doing homework or drafting emails. In fact, they have seen this for about the past three years. So how could you expect anything other than a lack of concern? And without enough concerned people, how to generate enough political momentum for substantial policy changes? (notably, while the HF incident was given substantial news attention, my impression is that most people only engaged with it superficially and have since moved on).

It seems to me that we must find some way to reconcile this gap (ie making people more familiar with the jaggedness, the swarms of agent communications, chain of thoughts of long agentic sessions). I have no idea how. But on the current trajectory, I still find it likely people will just be completely surprised when something catastrophic does happen.
